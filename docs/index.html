Sandwich attacks on Elysium

Elysium opened as a private mainnet with early-access trading and went public on Nov 9 at 13:00. Bots have run more than 24,000 sandwiches since the private launch. After the public launch, the rate fell from about 160 sandwiches an hour to about 9.

What does a sandwich look like?

Both victims sent their buy to the sequencer, and in both the bot saw it before it reached a block. In 25% of sandwiches the victim sent its buy through the inbox on HyperEVM instead (example).

How did bots see the trades?

Elysium has no public mempool, yet bots saw victims' buys before they reached a block, in two ways.

Finding sandwiches

  1. In one pool: a buy, then other wallets buying in the same direction, then a sell by the first buyer of about what it bought (within 5%), with the sell's block at most 30 seconds after the buy's block. We call this pattern a match. Each buy and each sell joins one match at most: a sell takes the bot's earliest buy that is still free.
  2. The buy and the sell come from the same wallet, each victim from a different one.
  3. A buy, then others buying, then a sell is also what an ordinary trader does. What makes it a sandwich is that the bot knew the victim's transaction before it landed. 17% of matches carry their own proof of that (a match can carry more than one):
    • Minimum hit: 9.3% of matches. The victim received just above an amount written in its own transaction (by at most 0.01%): its minimum output, which the bot's buy was sized to reach. Ordinary buys in the same pools show this about 0.5% of the time. An output exactly equal to such an amount is an exact-output trade and does not count.
    • Ordered by tip: 1.5%. In one block, the bot's buy pays a higher tip than the victim and its sell no more than the victim.
    • Inbox: 7.8%. The victim's buy was waiting in the inbox on HyperEVM before the bot bought.
  4. Proofs also fire by chance, on about 0.5% of ordinary buys, so one proven match proves nothing. A match counts when its operator is proven. An operator is proven in one of two ways:
    • by proofs: 10 or more proven matches, and more of them than chance would give, with odds below 1 in 100,000;
    • by results: a profit in 90% or more of at least 100 matches. A bot that pays no tip, or sends its own buy through the inbox, leaves almost no proofs but still wins nearly every time. This shows what the wallet does, not that it knew the victim's transaction; no ordinary trader with 30 or more matches comes close.
    Either way it also needs:
    • at most 20 wallets, since a larger group is app users rather than one bot;
    • a profit over all its matches;
    • a profit in more than half of its matches. A trader that buys and sells quickly in many pools also fits the pattern, but loses more often than it wins.
    Then all of its matches count; 53% of counted sandwiches have no proof of their own. The remaining proven matches, 1.3% of all matches and spread over 203 operators, do not count. In a random sample of 30 sandwiches counted without a proof of their own, the victim lost in 97% and the bot also profited in 97%.

The first version of this report (Nov 10) required the sell within 2 blocks. Blocks came 1 to 9 a second, so that meant 0.2 to 2 seconds and missed bots that sold later.

Detecting the inbox route

A victim's buy came through the inbox on HyperEVM when the block that executed it read a new message from the inbox (the block header counts messages read). The message's time comes from its MessageDelivered log on Elysium's bridge contract on HyperEVM.

Losses and profits

  • Victims lost: every transaction that swapped in the pool from the bot's buy to the last victim is re-run in order, without the bot's buy, with eth_simulateV1 on the state just before the buy's block. Each victim's result is compared with what it really received.
  • Not measured: 7.7% of sandwiches. 3.7% are in pools of two tokens with neither HYPE nor USDC, so their loss has no price; for the other 3.9% the re-run failed, had more than 40 transactions to replay, or its result could not be valued. They count as zero, so the totals are a floor.
  • Bots kept: both bot transactions are traced with debug_traceTransaction, summing every HYPE, WHYPE and USDC movement in and out of the operator's addresses, minus gas. Swap events alone overstate it, because launchpad hooks take fees outside the event.

Grouping wallets into operators

A wallet joins the bot contract it calls for its trades. Bot contracts with the same code, once hard-coded addresses are blanked, are joined. An address that repeatedly receives most of an operator's sell proceeds joins as its profit address. A contract that 5 or more other wallets use for their own trades is a public app, not a bot.

Out of scope

  • Sandwiches where the bot sold more than 30 seconds after its buy; launch sniping, arbitrage and back-running.
  • Profit moved to an address that the transactions don't link to the operator, or kept in tokens other than HYPE, WHYPE and USDC.
