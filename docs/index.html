Sandwich attacks on Elysium

From the first trades to Nov 10, 10:37 UTC. Every address, transaction and block links to the Elysium explorer.

What a sandwich isWhenWhoVictimsWhereMethod
TL;DR

Between Nov 4 and Nov 10, bots sandwiched 13,036 trades on Elysium, 99% of them before the public launch. Victims lost $153.4k (2,101 HYPE) to worse prices; the bots kept $86.0k (1,178 HYPE) after gas, and the rest went to pool and launchpad fees. Three operators caused 86% of the losses, and 65% of the losses hit users of one trading-bot router. The large operators stopped within two hours of the public launch; small bots have taken $1,145 since.

1 · The mechanics

What does a sandwich look like?

One from Nov 8, 23:29 UTC, all three transactions in block 2,784,446↗. The bot sees the victim's buy before it is included, buys first to raise the price, lets the victim buy at that price, then sells into the price the victim pushed up.

Step 1 · bot buys

The bot buys with 9.3 HYPE, paying a tip 1 wei above the victim's, so it is ordered first.

Step 2 · victim buys

Step 3 · bot sells

The bot sells everything with a zero tip, so it lands right after the victim, and keeps 0.083 HYPE. The rest of the victim's loss went to pool and launchpad fees.

How are sandwiches found?

  1. In each pool: a buy, then other transactions buying in the same direction, then a sell by the first buyer of about what it bought (within 5%), all within 2 blocks.
  2. The buy and the sell come from the same wallet; each victim from a different one.
  3. Same block: 1,101 sandwiches, all counted. Bot sold 1–2 blocks later: 11,973, counted only for operators that profit from the pattern overall (11,935).

How do we know the bot saw the victim's transaction first?

Elysium has no public mempool, yet in 576 of 594 back-to-back cases the bot's buy pays a higher tip than the victim and its sell no more than the victim, usually the victim's tip plus 1 wei. In 1,101 of 1,101 same-block cases the sell was signed together with the buy (consecutive nonces). The largest bot's contract refuses to sell unless it gets back more than it paid, which only works if it already knows a buy will land in between.

How are losses and profits measured?

  • Victim's loss: the victim's exact transaction re-run with eth_simulateV1 on the state just before the bot's buy, compared with what the victim really got.
  • Bot's profit: both bot transactions traced with debug_traceTransaction; every HYPE, WHYPE and USDC movement in and out of the operator's addresses, minus gas. Swap events alone overstate it, because launchpad hooks take fees outside the event.
  • USD: each day's median HYPE price in Elysium's own USDC pools.

How are wallets grouped into operators?

A wallet belongs with the bot contract it calls for its trades. Bot contracts with the same code once their hard-coded addresses are blanked are joined. An address that repeatedly receives most of an operator's sell proceeds is joined as its profit address. A contract that 5 or more other wallets use for their own trades is a public app, not a bot.

Out of scope

  • Sandwiches whose legs are more than 2 blocks apart, launch sniping, arbitrage and back-running.
  • Profit moved to an address the transactions don't link to the operator, or kept in tokens other than HYPE, WHYPE and USDC.
