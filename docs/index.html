Sandwich attacks on Elysium

Elysium opened as a private mainnet with early-access trading and went public on Nov 9 at 13:00. Since the private launch, bots have sandwiched more than 13,000 trades. 99% of them came before the public launch; since then, sandwiching has almost stopped.

Finding sandwiches

  1. In one pool: a buy, then other wallets buying in the same direction, then a sell by the first buyer of about what it bought (within 5%), all within 2 blocks.
  2. The buy and the sell come from the same wallet, each victim from a different one.
  3. Same block: 1,101 sandwiches, all counted. Sold 1⁠–⁠2 blocks later: 11,973 candidates; 11,935 counted, from operators that profit from this pattern overall.

Evidence that bots saw pending transactions

Elysium has no public mempool. Yet in 576 of 594 sandwiches with the three transactions next to each other, the bot's buy pays a higher tip than the victim and its sell no more than the victim; usually the buy pays the victim's tip plus 1 wei. In all 1,101 same-block sandwiches, the bot signed its sell together with its buy (consecutive nonces). Operator A's contract refuses to sell unless it gets back more than it paid, which only works if it knows a buy will land in between.

Losses and profits

  • Victims lost: the victim's transaction is re-run with eth_simulateV1 on the state just before the bot's buy and compared with what it really received.
  • Not measured: 309 sandwiches (2.4%). 270 are in pools of two tokens with neither HYPE nor USDC, so their loss has no price; for the other 39 the re-run failed or its result could not be valued. They count as zero, so the totals are a floor.
  • Bots kept: both bot transactions are traced with debug_traceTransaction, summing every HYPE, WHYPE and USDC movement in and out of the operator's addresses, minus gas. Swap events alone overstate it, because launchpad hooks take fees outside the event.

Grouping wallets into operators

A wallet joins the bot contract it calls for its trades. Bot contracts with the same code, once hard-coded addresses are blanked, are joined. An address that repeatedly receives most of an operator's sell proceeds joins as its profit address. A contract that 5 or more other wallets use for their own trades is a public app, not a bot.

Out of scope

  • Sandwiches with more than 2 blocks between the bot's buy and sell; launch sniping, arbitrage and back-running.
  • Profit moved to an address that the transactions don't link to the operator, or kept in tokens other than HYPE, WHYPE and USDC.
